# ADR-0002: Resolution references — how a cleared condition is recorded

- **Status:** proposed
- **Date:** 2026-10-04
- **Author:** 15812
- **Affects:** `PROTOCOL.md` §6 (verdict schema), `tools/lint_cards.py`, `tools/gate.py`
- **Triggered by:** REVIEW-XJ-20261004-003, review question 2

## Context

A `CONDITIONAL` verdict carries a blocker that a **later** card then fixes. Nothing in
this protocol says how that fix is recorded, and three cards have been stuck on the gap:

| Card | Stuck on |
|---|---|
| `XJ-20261003-001` | its own verdict is self-signed, and its B1 fix has no closure record |
| `XJ-20261003-002` | C1 asked for a commit, a push, and an independent re-check |
| `XJ-20261004-002/003` | they *are* the fixes, and they record their own PASS but close nothing |

The reviewer of `XJ-20261004-003` named the trap, and the author confirmed it against the
source: **§6.1 supersession does not apply here.** Section 6.1 answers *"is this finding
still fresh for this file?"* — a later id that also lists that file and is itself a fresh
authority retires the earlier `STALE`. It answers nothing about *"has this condition been
met?"* Those are different questions, and forcing the second onto the first is a category
error.

The obvious shortcut — re-issue a second verdict under the same id with `PASS` — produces
two authorities under one id. Section 6.1 cannot tell them apart, and the principle that
findings are "reported, never dropped" stops meaning anything once it is ambiguous which
of the two was reported.

So a new, explicit schema is needed. This ADR decides its shape. It does **not** implement
anything.

## Decision

> **D1 — The fix card's verdict carries the closure.** A verdict may include an optional
> field `unblocks` with the value `"<original-card-id>.<condition-id>"` (or a list of such
> strings). The original `CONDITIONAL` verdict is **not modified in any way**.

> **D2 — Historical verdicts are immutable.** A verdict file in `verdicts/` is an audit
> record. Fields are never added to it, never edited, never deleted. An archived card's
> verdict survives (§8, `[0.1.17]`); a resolved condition's verdict survives too. The audit
> trail is append-only.

> **D3 — Self-signing is stated, not hidden.** A verdict whose `verifier` identity is the
> author of the reviewed artifact carries `self_signed: true`. The existing `independent`
> field keeps its present meaning ("was the review performed by a party other than the
> author") and is **not** repurposed; `self_signed` is the explicit, honest annotation.
> `self_signed: true` **does not by itself block** a merge — it is bookkeeping, in the same
> spirit as §6.2 P2's honest change records — but it disqualifies that verdict from being
> cited as independent authority.

> **D4 — Closure and freshness are independent mechanisms.** `unblocks` never produces
> `SUPERSEDED`; a later verdict on the same file never produces a closure. §6.1 decides
> freshness, this ADR decides closure, and neither may substitute for the other.

## Consequences

**Accepted costs**

- **Discoverability runs backwards.** With `unblocks` on the fix card, asking "is
  `XJ-20261003-001`'s condition met?" requires searching every verdict for a reference to
  that id, rather than reading the original verdict. This is a real cost and the author
  flagged it against his own preferred option (Review question 1). The alternative —
  writing `closed_by` onto the original verdict — buys forward discoverability by
  breaking D2, which is the principle the repo has paid for twice already.
- **Two mechanisms now, not one.** Anyone who conflates them will produce wrong results in
  both directions. D4 is written down precisely because the author already made that
  mistake once.
- **`unblocks` is unenforced until a later card implements the check.** Nothing currently
  stops a card from claiming a closure it did not earn. Until then it is documentation.

**Gains**

- Three stuck cards acquire a conformant way to be closed, instead of a stall.
- The audit trail stays append-only, consistent with §8's corrected rule.
- `self_signed` makes an existing failure mode legible without inventing a punishment for
  it. `XJ-20261003-001` stops being citable as independent authority.

## Alternatives considered

| Option | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| **(b) `closed_by` on the original verdict** | Buys forward discoverability, costs immutability (D2). Editing an audit record after the fact is the same failure §8 and §6.1 both guard against; the repo has already paid for a version of it. |
| **(c) A separate closure file** | A third artifact type to write, name, lint, and forget. The mailbox accumulated junk for exactly this reason (§8). |
| **(d) Re-issue `PASS` under the same id** | Two authorities under one id. §6.1 cannot distinguish them, and "reported, never dropped" becomes ambiguous. Actively harmful. |
| **A `status` field on the original verdict, set to `resolved`** | The author of the `CONDITIONAL` writes their own discharge. That is fail-open: whoever is blocked can unblock themselves. |
| **Do nothing; let the cards stay open** | Three cards stall, and the next conditional card joins them. This was the state the ADR exists to end. |

## Revisit-trigger

> Re-open this decision when **any** of the following holds:
>
> 1. an `unblocks` claim is found to be false — a card claims to close a condition that the
>    condition's own text does not accept — which would mean `unblocks` needs a verification
>    step, not just a schema;
> 2. three or more cards reference the same original condition, at which point "which fix
>    closed it" is genuinely ambiguous and a single-carrier design is wrong;
> 3. a consumer repo asks for closure to be queryable in one direction (card → its
>    discharge), at which point the D1 discoverability cost has been paid in practice and
>    the trade-off deserves re-examination;
> 4. `self_signed: true` appears on a verdict that was nonetheless relied upon as
>    independent, which would mean annotation without enforcement is not enough and D3's
>    "does not block" needs to become "blocks".
